What the first consultation covers

Menopause is about far more than hot flushes.

Sleep, mood, concentration, energy, joints, cycles, weight, libido — the changes are wide-ranging and easy to dismiss one at a time. A longer appointment gives them the attention they need together.

01

Your story, in full

What has changed, when it started and how it affects your days. Forty minutes means nothing is rushed or left out.

02

Evidence-based options, explained

Dr Ashwath talks through the care options that fit your history and preferences, and what the evidence says about each.

03

A plan and a follow-up

You leave with a clear plan, any tests that are useful, and a review booked so the plan can be adjusted as you go.

Questions before you book

The practical details.

Do I need a referral?

No. You can book directly with Dr Ashwath by phone or online.

Where in Australia can I be?

Anywhere. The consultation is by secure video, so you only need a phone or computer with a camera and a quiet place to talk. Appointment times are in Sydney time.

My blood tests were normal. Is it still worth booking?

Yes. Perimenopause and menopause are diagnosed mainly from your symptoms and history, not from a single test. A normal result does not mean nothing has changed.

What should I have ready?

A rough timeline of what has changed, any recent test results, and a list of what you are currently taking. If you keep notes on your cycle or sleep, bring them — they help.

Will the Medicare rebate apply to me?

Rebates for video consultations depend on eligibility rules that change from time to time. Reception will tell you where you stand before you commit to the fee.

Can I see Dr Ashwath in person?

Dr Ashwath consults by telehealth. Rosedale Medical Practice at 70 Castle Hill Road, West Pennant Hills, also offers in-person appointments with other GPs.
